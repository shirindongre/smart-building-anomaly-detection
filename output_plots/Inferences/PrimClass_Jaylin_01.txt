This plot tracks the model's reconstruction error (MSE) at every timestep across the full year of 2014–2015 for PrimClass_Jaylin.


What the plot shows:
The grey line represents how well the LSTM Autoencoder reconstructed each 24-timestep window. The red dashed line at 
MSE = 0.0214 is the anomaly threshold derived from μ + 3σ of training errors. The pink shaded regions are windows where 
reconstruction error exceeded this threshold — flagged as anomalies.


Key observations:
The baseline reconstruction error for this building sits consistently between 0.005 and 0.015 across the entire year, which is 
well below the threshold. This confirms that the model has successfully learnt the normal operational patterns of 
PrimClass_Jaylin — the regular daily energy cycles, weekend dips, and seasonal variations are all being reconstructed accurately.

There are four distinct spikes that cross the threshold — one around late April 2015 reaching 0.038 MSE, one around late June 2015
reaching 0.030 MSE, one in early September 2015 reaching 0.028 MSE, and the largest spike in late November 2015 reaching nearly
0.050 MSE. Each of these spikes is narrow and sharp rather than gradual, which tells us the anomalies are abrupt events rather 
than slow drifts. This is characteristic of equipment faults, sudden setpoint changes, or occupancy-driven operational shifts.

The fact that the error returns to baseline immediately after each spike confirms that these are isolated anomalous events rather
than permanent regime changes — the building returns to its normal operational pattern after each disruption.
