This histogram shows the statistical distribution of reconstruction errors across all windows, separating training errors (blue) 
from all errors (orange), with the threshold line in red.

What the plot shows:
The x-axis shows MSE values and the y-axis shows the density of windows at each error level. The blue bars represent only the 
training portion of the data; the orange bars represent the combined training and test errors.

Key observations:
Both distributions are right-skewed and peak sharply around MSE = 0.005–0.008. This is a healthy and expected shape for a 
well-trained autoencoder — the vast majority of windows are reconstructed with very low error, indicating the model has 
confidently learnt the dominant normal patterns.
The two distributions — train errors and all errors — overlap almost perfectly throughout the bulk of the distribution. 
This is a strong positive signal: it means the test data follows essentially the same statistical distribution as the training 
data, confirming that the model generalises well and has not overfit to the specific training windows.
The threshold at 0.0214 sits far to the right of the peak, in the extreme tail of the distribution. There are very few windows 
between 0.021 and 0.050, and virtually none beyond 0.050. This confirms that the μ + 3σ threshold is correctly calibrated — 
it captures 99.7% of normal behaviour under the Gaussian assumption and only flags the most statistically extreme deviations 
as anomalies.
The slight orange extension beyond the blue in the tail (between 0.025 and 0.050) represents the anomalous windows in the test 
set. The fact that this tail extension is small and sparse — not a secondary peak — confirms that anomalies are rare and 
genuinely distinct from normal behaviour, rather than a gradual continuum. This validates the unsupervised anomaly detection 
approach: the model has learnt a clean boundary between normal and abnormal without any labelled fault data.
